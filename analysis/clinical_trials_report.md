# Global Clinical Trials Analysis: Trends, Status, and Technological Frontiers

## 1. Executive Summary

This report delivers a rigorous analysis of the global clinical trial landscape for prosthetics based on data extracted directly from **ClinicalTrials.gov**. A total of **2,189 studies** were evaluated to determine overall study statuses, regulatory trial phases, geographic concentrations, and leading technological breakthroughs.

---

* The active clinical innovation pipeline (379 recruiting + 148 upcoming + 121 active = 648 studies) indicates high vitality in neuro-prosthetics, myoelectric interfaces, and advanced additive socket fabrication.
* A low termination rate (3.47%) reflects high patient adherence and critical therapeutic necessity among amputee cohorts.

## 5. Technological Frontiers in Prosthetic Care

Recent active and recruiting trials highlight five paradigm shifts in prosthetic research:

### 1. The Agonist-Antagonist Myoneural Interface (AMI / Ewing Procedure)
* **Pioneering Studies:** NCT06371209, NCT03882073 (*Brigham and Women's Hospital, MIT, Walter Reed*).
* **Mechanism:** Rather than severing muscles into disorganized scar tissue during traditional amputation, paired agonist and antagonist muscles are surgically coapted over a synovial pulley or synthetic interface. When the patient contracts the agonist muscle, the antagonist is passively stretched, activating natural muscle spindle mechanoreceptors.
* **Impact:** Restores biological proprioception and joint position sense to the central nervous system without requiring invasive brain-machine interfaces.

### 2. Osseointegrated Bone-Anchored Prostheses (BAP)
* **Pioneering Studies:** NCT06853938 (*University of Alberta, Glenrose Rehabilitation Hospital*).
* **Mechanism:** Direct surgical anchoring of a titanium fixture into the femur or humerus, which osseointegrates with native bone and protrudes through a stoma in the skin.
* **Impact:** Completely eliminates the prosthetic socket—the single greatest source of pain, heat, dermatological ulcers, and fit instability for amputees. Paired with Targeted Muscle Reinnervation (TMR), it enables high-fidelity multi-joint control.

### 3. Machine-Learning Electromyographic Pattern Recognition Controllers (PRC)
* **Pioneering Studies:** NCT07075042 (*VCU, Hanger Clinic, DOD*), NCT04272489 (*Coapt LLC*), NCT06886295 (*Scuola Sant'Anna, Italy*).
* **Mechanism:** Replacing traditional two-site direct threshold switching (DC) with multi-electrode arrays that decode user intent via supervised classification and regression algorithms.
* **Impact:** Greatly reduces cognitive workload and mental fatigue during fine motor tasks (e.g., Clothespin Relocation, Jebsen-Taylor hand function tests).

### 4. Direct Digital Socket Manufacturing & Objective Fit Measurement
* **Pioneering Studies:** NCT07491614 (*University of Washington*), NCT01155024 (*Ohio Willow Wood*).
* **Mechanism:** Replacing traditional manual plaster casting with handheld optical 3D surface scanning, automated algorithmic rectification, direct 3D printing/additive manufacturing, and dynamic pneumatic coupling test systems.
* **Impact:** Dramatically compresses fabrication timelines and establishes reproducible, quantitative fit tolerances.

### 5. Closed-Loop Sensory Feedback
* **Pioneering Studies:** NCT02189031 (*Louis Stokes VA Medical Center, Cleveland Clinic*).
* **Mechanism:** Non-invasive and transcutaneous tactor arrays that convey real-time tactile and pressure signals from sensors embedded in the prosthetic fingertips back to the residual limb.
* **Impact:** Enhances prosthesis embodiment, reduces phantom limb pain, and enables users to grasp delicate objects without visual reliance.
